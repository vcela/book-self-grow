01 · The Author

Meet Caden Morell —
a designer who learned
to listen to rooms.

Fifteen years of interior architecture, two earlier books in eleven languages, and a long sabbatical that re-arranged the brief.

Caden M.
Lucerne · 2026

Caden Morell spent fifteen years as an interior architect in Copenhagen and Kyoto before a sabbatical changed the brief.

After watching how subtraction — fewer objects, fewer obligations, softer light — could quietly reorganise the people who lived inside a space, he began applying the same craft to the inner architecture of attention.

The Unclutter Method is the distillation of that practice: part journal, part field guide, part design manual for a quieter mind. Caden lives by a lake outside Lucerne with his partner, a retired greyhound, and what he insists is exactly the right number of books.

02 · The Book

Six movements,
from noise to shape.

The Unclutter Method does not ask you to throw anything away. It asks better questions about what you keep, what you carry, and what your day is actually for.

I

Notice the noise

Map the small frictions you've stopped seeing — the ones that quietly tax every hour.

II

Name what's kept

Move every possession, commitment and habit through a single, gentle question.

III

Soften the edges

The rituals — morning, threshold, end-of-day — that hold a quieter life in place.

IV

Make room

Designing emptiness on purpose — the room, the calendar, the inside of the head.

V

Place the few

How a small number of carefully chosen things can carry an entire life.

VI

Live the shape

Maintenance, not finish line. A practice for re-clearing, season after season.

"A room that breathes will, in time, teach you to breathe. The work is the same in either direction."
— from the preface
